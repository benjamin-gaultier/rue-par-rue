# House numbers the cadastre has no point for

Why: 14 Cité sheets find no parcel because the ALPAGE address points lack their number, so their footprints are not drawn. 13 are sorted in the README: 5 on a side of the street with no point at all (rue Cocatrix n°1, 7, 11; rue Basse-des-Ursins n°12; rue Saint-Christophe n°2, 4), 5 in a gap of the numbering (rue du Cloître-Notre-Dame n°14, 16; rue Massillon n°2; rue des Trois-Canettes n°2; quai de la Cité n°27), 2 past the street's last point (rue Saint-Christophe n°20, rue Saint-Landry n°12). The 14th is not found yet.

Done when: each of the 14 has its parcel from a source (or a stated reason it has none), the ones found are fitted with every check, and the reason is recorded on each footprint (`match`).

Notes:
- A side with no point usually means those parcels carry their address on another street: look at the parcels along that side of the street and the address points they do have (corner houses on the rue de la Colombe or the rue des Marmousets for the rue Cocatrix). A parcel along the right side whose only point is on another street, and which no traced sheet holds, is a candidate; the fit and scale checks then decide.
- The gaps may be parcels merged by 1836 (two numbers on one house) or a number the cadastre skipped: compare the parcel widths with the sheets' street fronts ("voye publique … 9 m 15").
- Only one corrected number fits a parcel (rue Saint-Christophe n°20, "en fait 18", IoU 0.75): not enough to read that street the other way. Decide street by street, never sheet by sheet.
- Plans 8133 (rue Cocatrix n°16) and 8135 (rue de la Colombe n°10) look like the same drawing in two scans: check the originals before using either.
